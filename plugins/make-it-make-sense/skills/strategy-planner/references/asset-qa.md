# Asset QA — run before any asset is "done"

- [ ] **Header present.** The file opens with: what it is, audience, objective,
      primary message(s), done vs. outstanding.
- [ ] **Traces to the tactic map.** The asset has a row, and its dependencies exist.
- [ ] **Every promise points to something real.** Any referenced material, demo,
      report, or capability exists or is a tracked deliverable in the tactic map.
      Assets promising assets is the sneaky version of over-claiming.
- [ ] **No new claims.** Every sentence traces to the message set.
- [ ] **Secondary messages used as lines are ≤200 characters.**
- [ ] **Build-status rule followed** for this medium (SKILL.md).
- [ ] **No needless assumptions about the audience's business model** (e.g. "you
      sell hardware" when not every partner does). Frame around what's universal
      to the audience or around the value.
- [ ] **Describes the whole offering,** not just the part that's top-of-mind.
- [ ] **Understandable cold.** A reader who doesn't know the product can picture
      it. Short, but complete enough to grasp.
- [ ] **Trap audiences:** the felt pain comes before the features.

## When the owner's requested structure cuts against the strategy
Don't silently comply *or* silently override.
1. Flag the tension with a concrete recommendation.
2. Keep the change one-line revertible.
3. Record what diverged and why in the asset's own header.
The owner still decides — from the tradeoff, not by default.
