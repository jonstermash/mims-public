# Expanding a message into longer-form content

When you need more than a line (landing page, one-pager, email, deck, article),
expand *within* the message set — never invent new claims.

## The ladder — one message, three lengths
1. **One line:** the primary as-is (the outcome).
2. **Short (a paragraph):** primary as the opening claim → one or two secondaries
   as support → the action.
3. **Long-form:** primary is the thesis / headline; each secondary becomes its own
   supporting beat (a paragraph, section, proof point, or FAQ answer); a vision
   primary becomes the closing "where this goes" beat; end on the action.

The 200-character cap applies to secondaries *as messages*. In long-form, a
secondary can grow into a full beat — but that beat must add only evidence and
context for the same claim.

## Guardrails
- **No new claims.** Every sentence traces to a primary or secondary already in
  the set. Need something new? Add it to the message set first — and verify it.
- **Outcome stays first;** the "how" and the proof stay underneath.
- **One audience per asset** — don't blend voices.
- **Length adds evidence and clarity, not hype.**
- **Carry the vision thread** where it fits, as a confident whole. Roadmap flags
  stay in the strategy doc, not in the copy (build-status rule, SKILL.md).

## Spoken / timed assets (pitch, talk, demo intro)
Budget by time, not page space.
- **Convert length to time against a stated pace:** ~150 wpm conversational,
  ~165 wpm energetic pitch. A 2-minute pitch is ~250–300 words — count them.
- **Pre-mark revertible "safe drops":** lines a speaker can cut live to reclaim
  ~5–10s each without losing a beat.
- **Structure in beats with time estimates** (~15s each). Keep rhythmic runs
  (a first/second/third product walk) crisp — that's where overruns hide.
- Live founder narration may name build stages (build-status rule, SKILL.md).
